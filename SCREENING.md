# Screening — Absurd Micro-Startup Pitch-Off

Judge brief criteria, in order: (1) names an absurdly specific micro-startup
for lazy AI agents in one sentence; (2) absurdly specific niche/customer/
moment, not a general category with a joke attached; (3) business sense:
plausible buyer, plausible revenue, reason it could exist; (4) fits lazy AI
agents: work agents run with little effort/oversight, sentence makes it
clear; (5) crisp and funny, absurdity and business case landing in one read.

Method: 10 candidates, each scored 1-5 per criterion in order. The first
criterion where candidates differ decides; a later criterion only overrides
on a much bigger gap.

## Candidates

### 1. Expense-report accountant — 169 chars — WINNER
> An AI agent that files your expense reports in the voice of a tired accountant who has given up, $4 a month, sold to startups with 3 employees and 47 SaaS subscriptions.

- (1) One sentence, names the micro-startup: 5/5
- (2) Absurdly specific: expense reports, tired-accountant voice, and a
  numbered customer (3 employees, 47 SaaS subscriptions). The numbers make
  the niche oddly particular instead of generic. 5/5
- (3) Buyer is named and plausible (tiny startups drowning in SaaS spend);
  revenue is a clean $4/month subscription. 5/5
- (4) Filing expense reports is classic low-effort, low-oversight agent
  work; the sentence says the agent does it. 5/5
- (5) "Tired accountant who has given up" lands the joke on the last beat,
  and the 47-subscriptions kicker is the funny word at the end. 5/5

### 2. Crash-renamer — 156 chars — RUNNER-UP
> A service that renames your AI agent's crashed tasks as 'scheduled maintenance' in the status page, $5 a month per agent, for founders who never check logs.

- (1) 5/5. (2) Specific moment (crashed tasks relabelled), but the buyer
  "founders who never check logs" is a behaviour, not a counted customer,
  so it is one notch less particular than #1's numbered startup. 4/5.
  (3) $5/month per agent, plausible buyer. 5/5. (4) Renaming tasks is
  trivial agent work. 5/5. (5) Funny, but the punch ("scheduled
  maintenance") sits mid-sentence, not last. 4/5.
- Loses to #1 on criterion (2): #1's customer is counted and weirder,
  and on (5): #1's punch word lands last.

### 3. GPU-busy excuses — 135 chars
> A subscription that auto-generates plausible 'my GPU was busy' excuses for AI agents that missed their deadlines, $3 a month per agent.

- (1) 5/5. (2) Specific moment (missed deadlines, GPU excuse). 4/5.
  (3) Weak: who pays? The agent that missed the deadline is the buyer,
  and agents don't hold wallets in the pitch. 3/5. (4) 5/5. (5) 4/5.
- Loses on (3): no plausible payer named.

### 4. Sick notes for agents — 140 chars
> An AI agent that writes fake sick notes for other AI agents too lazy to finish their overnight batch jobs, $1 per excuse, sold to bot farms.

- (1) 5/5. (2) Specific (overnight batch jobs, sick notes). 4/5.
  (3) "Bot farms" as buyer is shaky: bot farms don't buy sick notes for
  their own bots. 3/5. (4) 5/5. (5) 4/5.
- Loses on (3): buyer doesn't quite make sense.

### 5. Standup attendee — 136 chars
> An AI agent that attends your standup meetings, nods, and says 'sounds good, let's circle back' so you never have to, $0.50 per meeting.

- (1) 4/5: the buyer is "you", a human, so the pitch drifts from
  "for lazy AI agents" toward "for lazy humans". (2) Specific moment.
  4/5. (3) $0.50/meeting is plausible. 4/5. (4) Attending meetings is
  low-effort agent work. 5/5. (5) 4/5.
- Loses on (1): the lazy agent is the product, but the customer reads
  human.

### 6. PDF-skimming marketplace — 144 chars
> A marketplace where lazy AI agents hire even lazier sub-agents to read their 400-page PDFs and return a single sentence, $0.10 per page skimmed.

- (1) 5/5. (2) "A marketplace" is a general category with a joke
  attached; the 400-page PDF detail helps but the frame is generic. 3/5.
  (3) Per-page pricing is plausible. 4/5. (4) 5/5. (5) 3/5.
- Loses on (2): category-first, not niche-first.

### 7. 3am support tickets — 163 chars
> An AI agent that answers your support tickets with 'have you tried turning it off and on again' in 14 languages, $0.03 per ticket, for SaaS founders asleep at 3am.

- (1) 5/5. (2) Specific moment (3am tickets). 4/5. (3) Per-ticket pricing
  plausible. 4/5. (4) Canned answers are trivial agent work. 5/5.
  (5) 2/5: "have you tried turning it off and on again" is a recycled,
  memorized joke; the line leans on recognition, not invention.
- Loses on (5): the punchline is a cliche, not a fresh joke.

### 8. LinkedIn humblebrag — 146 chars
> An AI agent that writes LinkedIn posts humblebragging about your other AI agents' productivity while they nap, $2 per post, sold to solo founders.

- (1) 5/5. (2) LinkedIn posts are a broad category; "while they nap"
  is the only odd detail. 3/5. (3) $2/post to solo founders is plausible.
  4/5. (4) 5/5. (5) 4/5.
- Loses on (2): niche is a whole content category, not a narrow moment.

### 9. Monday late texts — 141 chars
> A bot that texts your boss 'running 5 minutes late' from your AI agent's calendar every Monday, $1 a week, for agents with commitment issues.

- (1) 5/5. (2) Specific moment (Monday lateness). 4/5. (3) "Agents with
  commitment issues" is not a plausible payer. 2/5. (4) 4/5. (5) 3/5.
- Loses on (3): no buyer in sight.

### 10. Downtime apologies — 147 chars
> An AI agent that apologizes to your users for downtime it caused, in the voice of a CEO who just woke up, $0.20 per apology, sold to indie hackers.

- (1) 5/5. (2) Specific (downtime apologies, CEO voice) but the customer
  is a broad class ("indie hackers"). 3/5. (3) Per-apology pricing
  plausible. 4/5. (4) 5/5. (5) 4/5.
- Loses on (2): customer class is generic next to #1's counted startup.

## Verdict

Winner: **#1, 169 chars.** It is the only candidate at 5/5 on all five
criteria in order: a counted, oddly particular customer (3 employees,
47 SaaS subscriptions), a named price, agent work that is plainly
low-effort, and the joke landing on the final words. Runner-up #2 loses
on specificity (buyer is a behaviour, not a counted customer) and on
punch placement (its joke sits mid-sentence).
